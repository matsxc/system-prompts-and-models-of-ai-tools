# Sinगली, Condensed Brand Brief for the Web Design Team

Prepared from seven source documents. See the source list and read status at the end of this file.

## 1. What Sinगली Is

Sinगली is a fashion label built on a single gap. India's master embroiderers, weavers and tattoo artists are hired as nameless labour, paid by the piece, credited nowhere, while their work either disappears into fast fashion or resurfaces on a heritage shelf priced at eighty thousand rupees and up, artisan still a footnote. Sinगली builds in the empty middle: pieces priced around three to five thousand rupees, made in small numbered drops, where the maker is named co-creator on the tag and paid a share of what they made. VERBATIM: "Sinगली is that lane, in cloth." The legal entity is House of Singully Private Limited, based in Hyderabad. The name is written Sinगली, never Singali or Sin Gali.

Naming and dual-script rules. The name fuses Latin "Sin" with Devanagari गली (lane), called a language, not a logo. The Devanagari half is never translated, by absolute rule. The label's dual-script wordmark is its single most defensible asset and stays fused wherever the label speaks. The legal parent, House of Singully, is Latin-only, so the dual-script trick stays unique to the label. Whether the House and label ultimately share one constant mark or run separate marks is not yet settled, see Section 10.

## 2. Founder, Company, City, Status

The brand keeps its founder faceless by design; the artisan is the intended face, not a person behind it. The legal signatory on file is Maimona Chauhan. House of Singully Private Limited is incorporated (7 July, per the most recent source) with a studio identified in Hyderabad. Whether trademarks are actually filed is contradicted between sources, see Section 10. A creative director (Istituto Marangoni-trained) and a Design and Production Head are coming on, onboarding underway as of early September 2026. The first drop, one maker and ten pieces, fully documented from the first stitch, is in development but not shipped; no artisan or drop date is named in these documents.

Financial shape, from the flagship deck: bootstrapped on fifteen lakh rupees of founder capital, a twelve month breakeven target, and a profit target of thirty to forty lakh by month thirty six. The most recent source, the Phase A Questionnaire (2 September 2026), reveals the real scope is larger than one label: House of Singully is planned as a multi-segment culture company spanning fashion, a cafe (Sin Cafe, market study on file, go or no-go at month six), AI, talent management, events and party buses. Only fashion and cafe are near-term; the rest remain ideas with no operating plan.

## 3. Product

Categories and crafts named across sources: apparel and accessories built from upcycled denim, transformed carpet, heavy embroidery, and tattooed footwear or leather. The making philosophy, VERBATIM: "We don't manufacture. We transform." The brand starts from a canvas that already has a life, old denim, a carpet, a plain garment, a pair of shoes, and a maker's hand turns it into something unrepeatable. VERBATIM: "Roughly thirty percent of a piece is the canvas. Seventy percent is the soul."

The first drop is one maker, ten pieces, numbered, then retired, never restocked. A re-edition needs eighteen months minimum, a deliberate decision, and a new creative element. Drop sizes grow: ten at launch, fifteen to twenty-five early, fifty to one hundred by Year 2. Six product filters, in order: a named human always; human creative ownership (the test is ownership of the creative act, not whether it was handmade, so machine-assisted and print-on-garment work both qualify if a named person owns the idea); made with integrity; the canvas earns its place; priced with conviction; a story worth wearing. Three rules are absolute: no replicas, no discounting ever, no anonymous pieces.

Price point is the sharpest open contradiction in the record. The Primer and both creative briefs state three to five thousand rupees as a fixed conviction, not a business compromise. A later Unit Economics document recommends 6,500 rupees to hit breakeven. The most recent source, the founder's own answer in the September 2026 questionnaire, proposes four tiers instead: roughly 799 to 1,299, 3,500 to 5,000, 6,000 to 8,000, and 10,000 only if the piece justifies it, with a possible 4,000 to 6,000 band for a full look. Unresolved, see Section 10.

## 4. Artisan Model

Sourcing: the content pillar "The Search" is the artisan hunt itself, real and reel-led, documenting how a maker is found rather than announcing a finished collaboration. No sourcing method, region or specific artisan is named beyond village-based weavers and embroiderers in general; the flagship deck's tag example, "Sinगली × MATS, 3/10," is illustrative, not a confirmed name.

Crediting: every piece carries a woven tag with the maker's name, piece number and mark. VERBATIM: "The tag is where credit becomes physical." VERBATIM: "The artisan is not our labour. The artisan is our co-creator."

Payment is the second sharp contradiction in the record. Three revenue-share figures appear across documents written within weeks of each other: 2 percent (project instructions), 8 percent plus a salary of 15,000 to 20,000 rupees (an AI handoff brief, 8 July, claiming it supersedes the 2 percent), and 5 percent on a 6,500 rupee salary (a Unit Economics document, 14 July). The flagship deck's polished copy, likely the oldest framing: "Two percent of every piece they touch, for the life of the piece. A salary first." The founder's most recent answer (2 September 2026) proposes dropping a flat per-drop share: pay a salary or project fee up front, and only grant a sales share once an artisan has completed three successful drops, a retention mechanic rather than a universal term. Not yet resolved, see Section 10.

## 5. Brand Soul, Voice, Register

Three words anchor the whole personality: Knowing, Still, Generous. VERBATIM: "Sinगली is a person who has already decided." It is deeply Indian without performing Indianness, cool toward the market, warm toward its people, artisans and community first.

Register comparisons named in the sources: it shares a price band with Bluorng, Urban Monkey and Snitch, three brands credited with proving the buyer and price point exist, but none with a named maker, a real revenue share, or the dual-script mark. Explicitly not Fabindia's energy, called "slightly museum-like, slightly too reverent," and not the studio gloss of Sabyasachi or Zara. Visual references to argue with, not copy: Psychopath Lab, Almost Gods, MSCHF, Supreme. Note: A24 does not appear anywhere in the seven sources reviewed.

What it never does: no discounts or sales, ever; no replicas; no anonymous pieces; no translation of the Devanagari; never more than one exclamation mark, usually none; and it never uses luxury, exclusive, curated, premium, artisanal, "handcrafted with love," "limited edition" as hype, "get yours before it's gone," or "dropping soon." The house-level word ban, per the September 2026 answer, is shorter, just premium, luxury and curated. The voice test, VERBATIM: "Is this informing someone, or making them feel something? If it informs, rewrite it. If it makes them feel, publish it."

## 6. Visual World

Palette guidance is the third, most consequential contradiction; see Section 10 for both systems in full. The typography device unique to the brand is the शिरोरेखा (shirorekha), a structural horizontal hairline sub-elements hang from, echoing how Devanagari letters hang from their headline bar; it is being promoted to a load-bearing constant meant to survive the brand into non-fashion categories. The Latin letterform for "Sin" is not finalised; candidates include Boreck, Kaftan, Cooper Black paired with Inter, and others still unlicensed or untraceable. The Devanagari half is being custom-drawn to match the chosen Latin face rather than borrowed, so the two scripts read as one hand.

Photography: documentary texture over studio polish, real light and hands, the maker's dignity over the product's gloss, editorial over promotional. Rejected: blue-toned craft photography, luxury's visual language as it exists today, festival-palette shortcuts, ethnic-costume clichés, "anything that could belong to ten other brands." The feed is static-dominant; reels run only when content genuinely demands movement. Four content pillars: The Search (artisan hunt), The Knowledge (fragments, not lectures), The Object (the piece in its world), The World (still developing).

Packaging: the woven tag is treated as part of the piece, not packaging around it. VERBATIM: "The tag is not packaging. It is part of the piece." Motifs to avoid: gold as a flat screen colour (reserved for physical materials, foil, thread, hardware, where it reads real instead of cheap), faux-Devanagari display type standing in for the real script, and moodboards built from other fashion brands.

## 7. Customer

The audience is named गली Folk, a tribe, not a target market: young Indian aspiration-buyers who refuse mass fashion, are not traditional luxury consumers and not trend-followers, who read the tag before they buy, and tell one person about a piece rather than broadcast it. VERBATIM: "They already sense something is wrong with how fashion is made, and they have not yet found a brand that agrees with them completely." They already spend in the three-to-five-thousand-rupee band, just on brands with none of this soul, not looking for Sabyasachi and not looking for Zara, wanting something "Indian without being ethnic, considered without being cold, elevated without being inaccessible." The first 500 buyers are the founding गली Folk, each earning a permanent founding page on the website, a record, not a loyalty programme.

## 9. Website-Relevant Facts

Founding गली Folk, the first 500, are promised "a permanent founding page on the Sinगली site," a record, not a loyalty programme, a concrete feature to design for. The multi-brand collaboration concept has its own home: "a part of my website will be for this multi brand work." No document mentions Shopify or any e-commerce platform, that choice appears open. An email, hello@singully.com, appears once, tied to a future workspace, not a confirmed domain. Instagram is the primary channel referenced throughout (chats and collections named Singully.official and Singully.in feed the brand's own inspiration library), but no single account is confirmed as the final public handle. Sin Cafe is a named, near-term sibling venture with its own market study, not yet part of the fashion site's scope. Drop cadence: one core drop roughly every six weeks in Year 1, about eight total, with drop day defined narrowly, a single post, a link in bio, nothing else. At the end of the current House branding phase, the founder's stated deliverable order is a brand board first, a brand book second, production assets third, and a live web page only "if you will send it," so the website is real but not yet a confirmed near-term deliverable.

## 10. Open Questions and Contradictions

Palette. The Primer calls for restraint, two colours, generous space, no shouting, built on a near-black and an ivory ground. The Phase A Questionnaire (2 September 2026, most recent and most technically detailed) instead documents a five-colour "Indian Heritage" kit sourced from a screenshot: a deep red (flagged to be replaced, currently reads berry not blood), a dusty rose with no assigned job yet, a near-black and ivory that functionally replace the old ink and bone, and a gold ruled material-only since it fails contrast as a flat screen colour. The five-colour kit is the live, more recent direction; the Primer's two-colour language should be treated as superseded pending sign-off.

Price band. Three to five thousand rupees is a fixed brand value in the Primer and both creative briefs; a Unit Economics document recommends 6,500; the founder's own September 2026 answer proposes four tiers instead (roughly 799 to 1,299, 3,500 to 5,000, 6,000 to 8,000, up to 10,000 if justified). Left explicitly unresolved by the most recent source.

Artisan revenue share. Three figures on file within weeks of each other: 2 percent, 8 percent plus salary, 5 percent plus salary. The founder's most recent answer proposes dropping a flat per-drop share for one earned only after three completed drops with an artisan. Unresolved.

Trademark status. The Primer and flagship deck both state the wordmark and device mark are filed. The September 2026 questionnaire rules the opposite, "the original filing was never run," and treats trademark as not yet a constraint. The questionnaire is the most recent and most authoritative source here.

Scope of the brand. The Primer and both creative briefs frame Sinगली as a fashion label, full stop. The September 2026 questionnaire reveals House of Singully as an intended multi-segment culture company, fashion only the first branch. Confirm with the client whether the near-term site is fashion-only or must anticipate the wider House.

Founder identity and role. Sources disagree on whether the founder is a director at all, and the most recent answer leans toward an intentionally masked, mystery-driven public persona rather than a resolved corporate role. Unresolved, and likely outside a website copy brief, but the web team should not assume a named, public founder figure.
